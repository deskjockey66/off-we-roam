These are suggested return-visit plans built around **Namba in Osaka** and **Asakusa in Tokyo**. They are accommodation areas, not a claim about the exact hotels we used. I would choose a room on a quieter street within a manageable walk of the station, then check the route with luggage before booking. Staying close to the busiest signs is less useful if nobody sleeps well.

Namba puts the southern Osaka districts within reach. Asakusa gives the Tokyo stay a neighbourhood to return to, with Sensoji and the river nearby. Neither base makes every attraction a short journey. The point is to choose one place in each city and accept the travel time when heading elsewhere, rather than move hotels to chase every stop.

For fourteen days, I would use six nights in Osaka and seven in Tokyo, leaving on day fourteen. That gives each base enough time to become familiar and keeps the travel day separate from the main outings. The extra week is useful for slowing down, not only adding more destinations.

## Days 1–6: Stay around Namba

**Day 1:** Arrive and settle in. Buy anything needed for the next morning, then choose dinner close to the room.

**Day 2:** Explore Namba and Dotonbori without a timed booking. Leave room to stop for food rather than treating the streets as a route to finish.

**Day 3:** Choose the castle park and, if you want it, the separately ticketed castle museum. Keep the rest of the day flexible.

**Day 4:** Make one Kansai day trip. I would choose either Nara or one manageable Kyoto area, not try to cover both. Check the actual rail route from the accommodation and the walking involved. Return to the Osaka base for the night.

**Day 5:** Stay closer to Namba. Shinsaibashi is a possible shopping walk, or use the day for a slower start and anything you want to revisit. This is also useful space if the earlier weather changed the day trip.

**Day 6:** Pick one Osaka activity the family actually wants, or leave the morning free. A full theme-park day can replace this flexible day if it is a priority, with the additional ticket and journey planned. Pack without making the evening another major outing.

## Day 7: Change bases

Take the train from Shin-Osaka to the Tokyo area and continue to Asakusa. Reserve seats together. Buy the bento and drinks before boarding, allowing enough time to find the platform first. I would make the evening about settling in rather than a cross-city dinner reservation.

## Days 8–13: Tokyo, with a Yokohama day

**Day 8:** Keep Asakusa local, with Sensoji and the surrounding streets. It is a good first full day without a complicated transport plan.

**Day 9:** Choose Ueno, with a park walk and at most one museum that interests you. Check the museum's closing day and exhibition ticket. Avoid planning as if every building is open daily.

**Day 10:** Visit Yokohama for the Cup Noodles Museum, on a confirmed operating day. Arrange the desired factory activity and consider Chinatown afterwards. They are separate stops, so leave travel time between them.

**Day 11:** Have a slower day around the base. Kappabashi or another local walk can be enough. This day also lets you move an earlier outdoor plan if the weather required it.

**Day 12:** Choose a western Tokyo area, such as Shibuya, and make it the main destination. A booked observation deck is optional rather than compulsory. Leave time for the train journey back across the city.

**Day 13:** Revisit a favourite or choose another single district. I would keep this movable instead of saving the one activity we most wanted for the last chance to do it.

**Day 14:** Depart. Plan the airport connection around the actual flight, with packing and the family's pace included.


## The transfer and the money

Use **Shin-Osaka**, not Osaka Station, for the Tokaido Shinkansen. Book seats together and include the local connection from Namba. At the Tokyo end, choose Tokyo or Shinagawa based on the onward route. Asakusa is a further local journey, so leave room for that before expecting to be checked in.

For a starting allowance, use ¥15,000–16,000 per adult for one Ordinary reserved-seat intercity journey, then obtain the actual fare. That is a rounded budget, not a quoted ticket price. A child needing a reserved seat may need a child ticket; check the category rather than assume the adult fare covers the family. Food, accommodation and local transport are additional.

I would not automatically buy the national Japan Rail Pass for this route. Compare the individual journeys first. Two city bases and one intercity transfer rarely justify a national pass simply because the holiday lasts a week or longer. Our [Shinkansen guide](/stories/shinkansen-osaka-tokyo/) covers booking, luggage and the mountain-facing seats.

For the museum day, two adult admissions and three custom cups currently total ¥2,500 when the child qualifies for free museum entry. That example excludes food and other activities. Check the exact activity booking and the date calendar in our [Yokohama guide](/stories/yokohama-cupnoodles-museum/).

## Keep the practical bookings ahead of the rest

Reserve the important activity first, then move the flexible days around it. Cup Noodles Museum normally closes on Tuesdays, with holiday exceptions. Do not fix a Tuesday museum day from this sequence without checking. Any paid observation deck, special exhibition or theme park should have its own opening and ticket check.

These plans assume arriving in the Osaka area and leaving from the Tokyo area. If your flights require returning to Osaka, add the second train journey and the time it takes. I would not silently squeeze that return into an already full departure day. International flights and accommodation prices depend on your actual dates, so they are not hidden inside a made-up total here.
