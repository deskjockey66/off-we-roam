The Drake and Sheepdog would be near the top of my list for a drink in Canmore. They offer different reasons to go: the Drake for local beers and a pub setting, and Sheepdog for exploring smaller-batch beer, including the more niche lagers when they are on the rotating board.

This is a short list to help choose an evening. Start with the setting you want, then check the current menu and what is pouring.

## The Drake: start with the local beers

The Drake is the place I would mention first for a pub visit and local beer. If you know it as the Drake Inn, look for the pub beside the accommodation. The current restaurant and brewery branding is The Drake Screaming Retriever, which is useful to know when searching for the menu or directions.

It is at 909 Railway Avenue. The official site lists its own beers and current events, so I would check that before choosing the night. A quiet meal and a live event are different kinds of evening, particularly if you are travelling as a family. Choose the one you actually want rather than arriving with the wrong expectation.

I would ask what local beers are pouring and start there. The local beer option and the pub setting are the reasons it makes my shortlist. The [current Drake site](https://www.drakecanmore.ca/) is the place to check the menu and event calendar.

## Sheepdog: a good reason to look beyond the centre

Sheepdog Brewing is the other notable stop I would put near the top. I would go looking for the smaller-batch choices and the niche lagers when available. The tap selection rotates, so keep an open mind rather than counting on a particular beer being available.

The Canmore taproom is at unit 112, 105 Bow Meadows Crescent. That puts it away from the main downtown strip. Plan how you will get there and back, especially if you have already spent the day driving around the mountains. It is an evening worth organising on its own terms rather than adding as a last stop when everyone is tired.

There is also a Sheepdog location in Banff, and the policies differ. The published terms identify the Canmore venue as all ages and the Banff location as adults only. Check the current policy and any specific event restrictions if you are bringing children. An all-ages policy is useful information; it is not a promise that every event suits a family.

Use the [Sheepdog website](https://www.sheepdogbrewing.com/) for the current beer list and hours. I would ask what is on rather than assume the lager selection stays the same through the season. That changing choice is part of the reason to consider a small brewery.

## Grizzly Paw: another option to compare

The Grizzly Paw is worth considering if you want another established Canmore beer stop. Check which venue you are booking or navigating to: the downtown pub and the brewery-side options are not interchangeable locations.

For someone staying centrally, the pub may fit more naturally into an evening in town. If you are interested in a brewery setting, look at the separate venue information and the current arrangements before travelling. I would decide the setting first, then check the menu rather than assume one search result covers every part of the business.

The [official pub page](https://thegrizzlypaw.com/pages/the-pub) is the useful starting point. It is also where I would check food options before making it the family's dinner plan.

## Wild Life: when you want a cocktail instead

Wild Life Distillery gives the evening a different direction if beer is not what you are after. Its tasting-room information is worth checking for a cocktail stop. Distillery visits and tours can involve different arrangements, so read the booking details rather than assuming you can turn up for either in the same way.

This is an option to investigate for a change from the pubs. Check the [official visit page](https://wildlifedistillery.ca/pages/visit-distillery), including the correct location and current opening times. If there is a particular experience you want, establish whether it needs booking before building the evening around it.

## What I would allow for spending

Without a current confirmed menu price for every venue, I would use a planning allowance rather than pretend to quote them. As an initial budget, allow C$10–14 for a beer and C$18–25 for a cocktail, then check the actual menu. Those are our suggested allowances in Canadian dollars, not verified prices or receipts from the venues above. Food, tax and any tip are additional considerations.

For two people ordering a beer each, that allowance means C$20–28 before the other costs. It is easy for a casual stop to become a more substantial evening once dinner is added. Decide whether you want a drink or a full meal, then budget for the version you actually plan to have.

I would not book accommodation based on being able to walk to one brewery without checking the route. Nor would I assume a lift home would be available the moment we wanted one. Make the transport part of the plan and leave the car if you are drinking. That keeps the evening simple.

## Make it fit the rest of the trip

During a Canmore stay, I would put a drinks outing on a quieter day in town. It would be more enjoyable than trying to fit it after a long day trip, particularly with a child. Check the venue's current rules, and choose the time and atmosphere that suit your group.

You also do not need to turn this into a trail of places to complete. The Drake and Sheepdog are my notable starting points. Pick the one that appeals, see what is available and enjoy the evening. The slower pace was one of the things we liked about being based here.
