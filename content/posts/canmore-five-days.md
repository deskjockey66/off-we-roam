Five days gave us enough reason to choose carefully. For a first visit, we would keep Canmore as the base, spend a full day around Banff and allow one longer outing. The temptation would be to fill every morning with a departure time. Having lived here for several months with our daughter, I would leave more breathing room than that.

This is the trip we would plan, rather than a claim that we followed this exact five-day sequence. It suits a family with a car who wants mountains and time together without moving accommodation each night. Autumn needs flexibility, particularly once the summer services end.

## Day 1: Arrive and stay in Canmore

Check in and deal with the practical jobs before heading anywhere else. Buy the food you will want for breakfast, then take a short walk through town. A coffee is enough of a plan for the afternoon. Blondies would be my pick for a latte; I have put the other options in our [Canmore coffee guide](/stories/canmore-coffee/).

Keep dinner close to where you are staying. After a travel day, I would rather get settled than add a drive to Banff just to say we had started sightseeing. If arrival is late, move the town walk to the next morning. There is no reason to start the stay already catching up with an itinerary.

## Day 2: Get to know the base

Spend the morning in Canmore. Choose a gentle waterside walk according to the conditions, with time to stop rather than a distance you have to finish. The views around town were a big part of what we liked about staying here. You do not need a major attraction booked to have a worthwhile morning.

In the afternoon, consider Quarry Lake or a suitable local trail if the weather and current advice support it. Grassi Lakes is another possibility, but check the Alberta Parks trail report first. The routes differ, and autumn ice can change the outing. I would not describe it as an automatic pushchair walk or assume every route was open.

A provincial pass may be required where you park outside municipal land. Check the exact destination before paying. Staying within the town does not itself require a Kananaskis Conservation Pass.

## Day 3: A full day around Banff

Make Banff the main outing. We would combine time in town with Bow Falls, then decide whether there was enough energy and daylight for a further stop. Lake Minnewanka could be an option when roads and conditions allow. It should stay optional rather than become the reason lunch and the walk through town get rushed.

Banff National Park admission is separate from the provincial Kananaskis pass. Carry what you need for the day, including extra layers, and leave some time for the return to Canmore. I would avoid booking an expensive evening activity after a full day out with a child. A simple dinner back at the base would make the day easier.

## Day 4: Choose the longer outing

Lake Louise is the obvious choice if it is a priority. Plan transport before leaving, and check the arrangements for your actual dates. The lakeshore and Moraine Lake have different access issues; they should never be treated as two stops you can simply drive between whenever you like.

For late autumn 2026, the Parks Canada lake shuttles finished on 12 October, and the resort summer lift finished on 11 October. Moraine Lake Road is closed to ordinary private cars. Do not book a late-October stay expecting the summer lake-and-lift day to operate. Our [lake planning guide](/stories/lake-louise-moraine-guide/) explains the seasonal distinctions.

If the lake outing does not work, keep the day closer to Canmore or choose an accessible Kananaskis stop after checking road and trail reports. Calgary is another option for a city day when mountain weather makes an outdoor plan unappealing. I would choose one of those directions, not combine them.

## Day 5: Leave some room at the end

Use the last morning for whatever you enjoyed most locally. It could be another coffee and a walk, or simply a slower breakfast before packing. This is also the spare day if the earlier weather made you swap plans. With only five days, having one movable part is useful.

If you are flying out, allow proper time for the journey and airport arrangements rather than treating departure day as another full sightseeing day. A final stop is pleasant only when it does not turn the end of the trip into a rush.

## What we would budget for separately

The following are published pass prices checked on 15 October 2026, in Canadian dollars. They are planning references, not a record of what our family paid for this suggested trip. Recheck the official pages for your visit.

| Item | Published price | How it affects this plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Banff family/group daily admission | C$24.50 | Two paid national-park days would be C$49 |
| Kananaskis vehicle day pass | C$15 | Only needed for applicable parked visits within the pass area |
| Kananaskis annual pass | C$90 | Usually unnecessary for this short plan unless further visits justify it |

Youth aged 17 and under have free national-park admission. The family/group rate is useful for two adults travelling together; check the rules for your own group. Accommodation, fuel and meals remain separate. I have avoided pretending there is one reliable nightly accommodation price for Canmore across the season.

Our remembered Fairmont lunch at Lake Louise was about C$60 for sandwiches and coffee. Treat that as a warning to allow for food, rather than the required lunch stop. Bringing your own lunch is a practical choice.

## What I would leave out

I would not add Fernie as a day trip. We stayed there for three nights in a large RV, and it made more sense as its own short stay. I would also avoid trying to reach Jasper and return within this plan. Five days can feel generous if you keep the area manageable, or very short if you spend most of them on the road.

Johnston Canyon is not included as a guaranteed stop: an official closure was issued on 13 October 2026. Check the live Parks Canada bulletin before considering a future visit. A familiar place appearing in another itinerary does not make it available for yours.

Useful checks before setting out: [Banff conditions](https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/ab/banff), [Alberta Parks](https://www.albertaparks.ca/) and the [Kananaskis pass](https://www.alberta.ca/kananaskis-conservation-pass).
